# Source Map — The Math Academy Way

This file provides provenance for the operational rules in this skill. It is for auditing and maintenance, not normal execution.

**Source used:** Justin Skycak, _The Math Academy Way: Using the Power of Science to Supercharge Student Learning_, first-edition working draft, updated 17 August 2026. Page numbers below refer to the page numbering in the book's own table of contents / document, not DOCX paragraph numbers.

## Status labels

- **Direct** — the operational rule is explicitly stated or clearly prescribed by the book.
- **Derived interpretation** — the rule is a synthesis or application inferred from multiple parts of the book; it is not presented as a direct quotation or single explicit rule.
- **Product-specific** — the source describes Math Academy's architecture/experience; the skill preserves the design idea without claiming universal validation.
- **Provisional** — the main source section is explicitly marked “In Progress” or appears in “Notes for Future Additions.”

The skill paraphrases rather than reproduces substantial passages.

## Important source tensions preserved in the skill

### T1 — Non-interference vs interleaving

- Ch. 17 says similar new associations can interfere and should be separated in time.
- Ch. 19 says mixed practice improves discrimination between problem types.
- FAQ Ch. 19 discussion acknowledges a small initial blocked dose can be useful when first learning.

**Resolution used by the skill:** separation during fragile initial acquisition, later contrastive interleaving after baseline mastery. This is marked **Derived interpretation**, not attributed as the author's explicit combined algorithm.

### T2 — Mastery before advancing vs automaticity as a gatekeeper

- Ch. 13 requires sufficient prerequisite proficiency before advancement.
- Ch. 15 emphasizes that advanced performance needs automaticity in foundations.
- FAQ explicitly rejects the idea that full automaticity is required before every next step.

**Resolution used by the skill:** advance at baseline mastery; build automaticity longitudinally through later spacing and layering. This follows the FAQ's clarification and avoids treating “mastery” and “automaticity” as identical.

### T3 — Scaffold heavily vs maximize difficulty

- Ch. 14 argues for fine scaffolding to prevent cognitive overload.
- Ch. 12 argues that high-efficiency deliberate practice should push near the learner's edge and can feel uncomfortable.

**Resolution used by the skill:** maximize difficulty subject to timely eventual success. Increase scaffolding when failure is caused by overload; remove it when it no longer contributes to success.

### T4 — Comprehensive curriculum vs streamlined goal paths

- Ch. 9 emphasizes comprehensive courses and warns against shallow acceleration.
- Ch. 21 and FAQ “holistic mode” argue against front-loading unrelated historical gaps.
- Ch. 32 explicitly distinguishes core and supplemental topics and describes a streamlined foundations sequence.

**Resolution used by the skill:** preserve the user's scope contract. Comprehensive course -> cover all promised material. Goal-directed path -> prioritize prerequisite closure of that goal and defer unrelated gaps.
